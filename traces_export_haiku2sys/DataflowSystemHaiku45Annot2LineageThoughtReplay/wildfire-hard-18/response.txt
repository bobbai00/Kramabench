The analysis controlled for weather variables (wind speed, relative humidity, fire danger index, and rainfall) and examined the relationship between aggressive suppression (Full Suppression vs. Other strategies) and three outcomes: fire duration and structures threatened (both log-transformed).

## Key Findings:

**Fire Duration:**

**Buildings Affected:**

- Aggressive suppression *increases* outbuildings threatened by approximately **110%** (coefficient: 0.7427, p<0.0001)
- This suggests fires affect **MORE buildings** with aggressive suppression ✗

## Interpretation:

This appears paradoxical: more aggressive suppression does end fires faster, but fires subject to aggressive suppression are associated with substantially more threatened buildings. This likely reflects **confounding by severity** — fires that are larger, more threatening, or in areas with more structures trigger more aggressive suppression responses in the first place. Even after controlling for measured weather variables, aggressive suppression appears to be deployed to more severe fires, resulting in more threatened structures.

**Final Answer: No, more aggressive suppression does not contribute to fires affecting fewer buildings.
